## Event and purpose (PDF pp. 1-4)

- Full name: SDC: Sustainably Developing Code Hackathon Competition.
- Organizer: XMUM AI Club. Collaborator: XMUM Rotaract Club. FAQ also mentions participating university collaborators.
- Hybrid AI hackathon for solutions addressing real-world challenges aligned with United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs 1-17).
- Online phase: 19-30 October 2026. Physical hackathon starts 31 October at Xiamen University Malaysia (XMUM); pitching and closing are 1 November.
- Teams have 1-5 members; solo entrants and teams across universities are allowed.
- Objectives: translate academic knowledge into deployed applications through workshops and mentorship; solve SDG-related challenges with AI; connect universities, industry mentors and sponsors; develop creativity, design thinking, critical analysis and solutions with social or economic impact.
- Cover contact details: Instagram `@sdc_xmumhack`; email `sdc.xmumhack@gmail.com`.

## Document authority and future operational details (PDF pp. 5-6)

- Registration constitutes agreement to the official Terms and Conditions (T&C). Participants must read and comply with them.
- T&C cover registration, eligibility, team formation, submissions, judging, plagiarism, disqualification, conduct, IP, media consent and other competition matters. T&C prevail over inconsistent handbook provisions.
- This Official Handbook was released before registration as a high-level overview for prospective entrants.
- A separate Participant Handbook is to be issued after registration closes. It provides domain problem statements, judging weightage/scoring, prize distribution by placement/domain, physical-phase meals/logistics, sponsor terms/datasets/resource access and operational details not finalized in this version.
- The Participant Handbook governs event operations where inconsistent with this Official Handbook; this Official Handbook and T&C continue to govern eligibility, conduct and policy. The stronger T&C precedence statement still applies.

## Eligibility, teams and fees (PDF pp. 7-12)

- Entrants must be officially enrolled at recognized Malaysian higher education institutions and remain enrolled throughout the competition. Explicitly listed programs: foundation, diploma and bachelor's degree. The more general phrase is "any degree"; no explicit postgraduate eligibility ruling appears here.
- One person may register with only one team. Entrants must be available for required online sessions, preliminary round and, if shortlisted, finals.
- Organizers, committee members, judges, mentors and other parties directly involved in organizing, planning, managing or administering the event cannot compete.
- Organizer may reject registrations that fail eligibility verification.
- Registration fee per team: RM40 early bird or RM50 normal, plus RM25 commitment fee. This implies initial payment of RM65 or RM75 respectively. No early-bird cutoff is specified in this handbook.
- Teams may mix universities. All members must obey XMUM campus rules, regulations and disciplinary guidelines during the physical event, respect facilities, staff, committee and fellow participants, and follow organizing instructions. Breaches may lead to penalties, disciplinary action or disqualification.
- Each team appoints one leader for registration, official announcements, team communication and final deliverable submission.
- All members must join the official Discord server.
- No member changes after registration is completed, including after the event begins.
- Unregistered people cannot substantially contribute to the project, prototype, presentation, documentation or final submission. Noncompliant team composition may make a team ineligible for judging.
- Commitment fee is refundable only after the event and subject to organizer verification. A valid preliminary submission must include a working prototype, complete code repository and all required documentation.
- No refund for no submission, incomplete, inaccessible, invalid or insufficient work.
- A shortlisted team that misses the physical event without at least 48 hours' prior notice permanently forfeits its RM25 commitment fee. Exact refund timing will be announced.
- To qualify as finalists, teams must complete preliminary phase, submit all deliverables on time, be shortlisted, confirm participation by the organizer's deadline and attend the physical final according to attendance requirements. An unconfirmed slot may be reassigned.

## Challenge scope and required result (PDF pp. 15-17)

- Two competition domains are stated, each with an organization partner. General objectives earlier say "two to three" domains; see inconsistencies below.
- Domains are randomly assigned during registration, are not self-selected, and cannot be switched after assignment (including after preliminaries start). There is no team cap per domain because assignments are random.
- Teams identify their own specific problem angle within the assigned sponsor-defined brief, demonstrating alignment with at least one SDG. Briefs, sponsor resources and evaluation notes are to be released via Participant Handbook and formally announced on 19 October.
- Solutions must be developed primarily during the official hacking period; pre-existing work follows Section 8.2 declaration and approval rules.
- All three are required: a working technical prototype, a live pitch/presentation and a functional user interface. Missing any makes the submission incomplete.

## Tools, pre-existing work and AI policy (PDF pp. 18-22)

- Free, open-source, sponsor-provided and paid APIs/cloud/development tools are allowed. Teams bear costs for paid APIs, platforms, storage, deployment and other services unless otherwise announced.
- Sponsor API keys, credits and resources must follow their limits/terms/instructions; teams cover usage beyond provided quotas.
- Declare all major external tools, APIs, cloud services, AI tools, datasets, models, sponsor resources and pre-existing material in the submission or documentation. Paid tools and major third-party platforms must also be clear. Failure may lead to point deductions, disqualification or other committee action.
- Pre-existing code, frameworks, models, templates or project materials created before the hackathon must be declared during registration and approved by the committee. Undeclared pre-existing work can violate originality and incur innovation/creativity deductions.
- Major service outages (examples: GitHub, AWS, OpenAI, Replit and other approved tools) may prompt deadline extensions or alternative offline evaluation at committee discretion, immediately announced on Discord. An extension is not automatic.
- AI code assistants, LLMs and AI development platforms may assist development and must be declared under Section 8.4.
- Code, content and assets generated during the official hacking period directly from a team's own prompts do not count as pre-existing work and need no separate Section 8.2 declaration/approval. AI-use declaration rules still apply.
- Permitted assistance includes code generation, debugging, documentation and design support. The team's own work must supply core problem understanding, solution design and technical decisions.
- Teams must explain and justify major components, including AI-generated code, design and content. Inability to explain substantial material may signal misrepresentation.
- A substantially AI-generated project with minimal team understanding, original input or modification can violate originality and cause deductions/disqualification at committee discretion.
- Acceptable-use standard: team members can explain why major technical/design decisions were made independently of AI suggestions; AI outputs have been reviewed, modified or adapted rather than pasted wholesale; core problem framing and approach arise from the team's understanding of the SDG/domain brief rather than an AI-generated project idea.
- Any member can be asked to explain any part, including AI-assisted portions. Lack of understanding of a substantial core algorithm, architecture choice or main feature may be flagged regardless of whether it was hand-written or AI-assisted.
- Participant Handbook may impose further guidance and domain-specific AI restrictions.

## Inconsistencies, caveats and unspecified matters

1. **Number of domains:** objective Section 2.2 says "two to three"; Section 7.2 explicitly states two. Treat the latter as more specific, subject to registered-participant materials.
2. **Shortlist size:** timeline says top 15 per domain; FAQ qualifies this as **up to 15**, subject to organizer confirmation.
3. **Eligibility wording:** Section 4.1.1 broadly says any degree at recognized Malaysian institutions, but Section 4.1.2 lists foundation, diploma and bachelor's only; FAQ broadly says current university/higher-education students. This does not settle postgraduate eligibility.
4. **Domain terminology:** Section 7.4.2 says "selected domain" even though Sections 7.1/7.3 make clear that allocation is random and locked. Do not infer permission to self-select or switch.
5. **Document references:** PDF p. 5 calls the handbook "Participant Handbook" in its T&C inconsistency sentence while surrounding text distinguishes this Official Handbook from the future Participant Handbook. The policy hierarchy is nevertheless explicit.
6. **Pagination:** two consecutive physical PDF pages (21 and 22) both print footer number 19. Subsequent printed page numbers are one below the physical page offset one would otherwise expect. Use physical PDF pages to find material.
7. **Fees:** RM25 is additional to RM40/RM50 registration; only commitment fee has a stated refund policy. Early-bird end date and precise refund-processing date are absent.
8. **Exact operational deadlines, rubric and prize split:** not set out here; explicitly deferred to Participant Handbook/Discord. The 25 October timeline marker alone is not an exact timestamp.
9. **AI nuance:** no separate pre-existing-material approval is required for in-period prompted AI outputs, but tools/resources must still be declared and outputs must be understood and adapted. Do not treat this exception as permission for an almost wholly AI-originated project with minimal team input.
10. **No formal numeric AI percentage limit**, minimum originality percentage, required framework, mandated programming language, or fixed general dataset is stated here. Domain rules can add constraints.
