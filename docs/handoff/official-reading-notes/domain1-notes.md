## Problem statement and rationale

Build an AI-powered solution that continuously tracks AI advancement, predicts emerging and declining jobs and skills, matches current workers and future talent to suitable future roles, and produces personalized reskilling pathways. It must show how each role and skill connects to the 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals. (PDF p. 2 / printed p. 1.)

The listed SDG targets are 4.4 (relevant skills for decent jobs), 4.3 (lifelong technical and vocational learning), 8.2 (productivity through innovation), and 8.5 (full and productive employment). (PDF p. 2.)

The document argues that AI changes roles more quickly than training programs and frameworks can adapt. It cites the Hays Asia Salary Guide 2025 for the claims that 56% of Malaysian professionals use AI tools at work and 26% received formal training. It also attributes to the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation the claim that over 600,000 workers need reskilling within three to five years. These are claims in the source PDF, not independently verified here. (PDF p. 3 / printed p. 2.)

The intended philosophy is AI as a partner supporting people. A one-time assessment or static learning list is insufficient; the system must keep responding as technology moves. Clean energy, health, smart agriculture, and responsible AI are examples linking future jobs to sustainability. (PDF p. 3.)

## Intended users

- Working adults at risk of job displacement.
- Fresh graduates and career switchers deciding their next direction.
- HR and learning-and-development managers planning team reskilling.
- TVET and university career centers advising students.

Source: PDF p. 4 / printed p. 3.

## Product and expected journey

A working web application or dashboard is expected; a proof of concept is acceptable. The experience should take a user from their current situation to plausible roles in the AI era. The typical journey is:

1. Enter skills, experience, and interests through a CV upload, form, or short questionnaire.
2. View a Future Skills Radar showing rising and declining skills and roles based on identifiable AI-trend and job-market signals.
3. Receive best-fit future role recommendations, each with a match score and a skills-gap breakdown.
4. Follow a step-by-step reskilling plan, with every recommended role and skill linked to one or more SDGs.

Source: PDF p. 4 / printed p. 3. The CV-upload option must be read together with the prohibition on using real CVs in the demo (PDF p. 6).

## Six must-have features

**Final product:** all six are mandatory. **Preliminary round:** the proposal must explain delivery of every feature, and the demo must show the most important feature working. (PDF p. 5 / printed p. 4.)

| Feature | Exact substantive expectation |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Skills profile intake | Capture current skills and potential using CV upload, a form, or questionnaire. |
| 2. AI-trend signal layer | Use at least one updatable source for AI and job-market trends. Examples are public job-posting data, news or research feeds, and open datasets. Show the source and its date. Hard-coded lists alone do not count. |
| 3. Future-role matching | Rank suitable future roles using an explainable match score and a clear skills gap. |
| 4. Personalized reskilling pathway | Provide an ordered learning plan with estimated time and free or low-cost resources. Online courses and HRD Corp-claimable programs are examples. |
| 5. SDG mapping | Tag each role and skill to at least one SDG, and explain how the selected career path contributes to the goals. |
| 6. Handling uncertainty | Show more than one future scenario, such as moderate versus fast AI disruption, and clearly identify predictions as estimates. |

## Optional enhancements

These are explicitly **not required and not scored separately**; they can strengthen Innovation & Creativity or Problem-Solution Fit. (PDF p. 6 / printed p. 5.)

- Employer/HR view with workforce skills-gap heatmap and team/company reskilling plan.
- Automatic replanning when changing AI-trend data changes recommendations.
- Inclusive design: English, Bahasa Malaysia, and Chinese; low-bandwidth and mobile-friendly experience.
- Learner motivation features such as progress tracking and skill badges.
- Fairness checks against discrimination based on age, gender, or background.
- SDG contribution aggregated for a person, company, or region.

## Rules and boundaries

- Use public, open, or synthetic data only. Do not use real personal data or real CVs. Create sample profiles for the demo.
- If a language model is used, disclose which one.
- Every recommendation must cite a source.
- The system must not invent jobs, courses, or statistics.
- Support human career decisions and judgment; do not make final hiring decisions.
- Technology stack is freely chosen.
- Full course-content creation, payment systems, and real recruitment integrations are out of scope.

Source: PDF p. 6 / printed p. 5.

## Success criteria

A new user should reach ranked future roles and an actionable pathway from their profile within minutes. Every recommendation must be transparent about its source. Results should visibly change when trend data changes. SDG connections must have substance and should affect understanding or choices, rather than act only as decorative badges. (PDF p. 7 / printed p. 6; final sentence paraphrases the stated meaningful-SDG requirement.)

## Ambiguities and information to resolve from other materials

- No scoring bands or band percentages are defined here, although the calculation refers to them.
- No actual proposal length, submission-file format, deadline, presentation duration, or demo duration is specified here.
- The final-round rubric is intentionally deferred.
- No minimum number of roles, skills, courses, users, or sources beyond at least one updatable trend source is specified.
- No required source API, refresh frequency, forecast horizon, model, scoring formula, or accuracy threshold is specified.
- The problem asks for continuous tracking, while automatic replanning is optional. A source that can update and outputs that visibly respond to changed data are explicit expectations; automatic scheduling itself is not expressly required.
- SDG icons highlight 4, 5, 8, 9, and 10, while the problem references the full set of 17. The explicit feature rule is at least one SDG tag per role and skill; it does not require every individual path to cover all 17.
- A synthetic CV-upload demo is compatible with the stated interface option and the data rule. Real CVs/personal data are explicitly prohibited for the demo.
- The brief permits synthetic data but separately requires an updatable AI/job-market source and dated sources for recommendations. A wholly fixed synthetic list would fail the stated hard-coded-list restriction.

These points distinguish document omissions and careful readings from additional rules; none introduces a new organizer requirement.
