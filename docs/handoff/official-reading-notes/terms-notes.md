## 1. Introduction, scope, definitions and objectives

**PDF pp. 2-5 / printed pp. 1-4, clauses 1.1-1.4.** Registering or participating constitutes acceptance of the terms for registration, development, submission, judging, the physical final, prizes, and related activities. Participants must read and understand them; noncompliance may lead to marks deductions, disqualification, prize forfeiture, or other organiser action.

The organiser is the SDC Hackathon 2026 Organising Committee. The event is organised by XMUM AI Club with XMUM Rotaract Club and supporting partners. It uses a learn-then-build format: online preliminary phase, online finalist upgrade phase, and physical grand final. The objective is practical AI solutions addressing real challenges aligned with SDGs 1-17, with collaboration, industry exposure, design thinking, and social/economic impact.

Definitions include team (1-5 registered participants), designated team leader, submission (source code, documentation, presentations, GitHub repo, demonstration videos and any further requested materials), finalist, sponsor resources (APIs, cloud credits, software licences, platforms, datasets and other support), and competition domain. **Each team enters only one domain; finalists are selected independently within each domain** (1.3.8).

## 5. Project development and permitted resources

**PDF pp. 17-25 / printed pp. 16-24, clauses 5.1-5.12.**

### Originality and timing (5.1-5.4)

Submissions must be original team work, substantially developed during the official period, with honesty, academic integrity, fairness and professionalism. Teams are responsible for third-party IP rights and may have to provide evidence, commits and development records (5.1).

Copied work, substantial similarity or reproduction from public sources without significant original contribution may disqualify a team. The organiser may use plagiarism tools, repo comparisons, searches and code similarity checks (5.2).

Projects substantially complete before commencement are ineligible. Teams must not submit projects previously submitted to another hackathon, entered in another competition, commercially deployed, publicly launched, or judged in a similar event. Repo creation dates, commits, timestamps and document histories may be checked; substantially similar pre-event projects can be disqualified (5.3).

Reasonable supporting pre-existing code is allowed if it is not the primary innovation: examples are auth modules, utilities, DB connections, UI components, logging, configuration. Cosmetic modification of an existing project is disallowed. Core idea, implementation, approach and major functions must be developed specifically for this competition. Significant pre-existing code must be declared when requested; nondisclosure can be misconduct (5.4).

### GitHub (5.5)

Every team must maintain an official GitHub repo created **only after commencement**. Recycling an earlier repo needs prior written organiser approval. Commits must be clear, traceable and accurately reflect actual development. No submitted-repo modification after the deadline unless expressly instructed; organiser may archive/clone for judging/verification. The numbering jumps from 5.5.5 to 5.5.7; no 5.5.6 appears in the PDF.

### AI (5.6)

AI tools are permitted and encouraged as productivity assistants. Genuine participant contribution remains required. Teams are fully responsible for generated code, technical correctness, originality, licence compliance, security vulnerabilities and ethical use. AI does not exempt plagiarism/IP/copyright compliance. Organiser may investigate “excessively or improperly” AI-generated submissions; no quantitative threshold for excessive use is defined.

### Open source, frameworks, templates, sponsor resources (5.7-5.9)

Public open-source libraries, SDKs, frameworks and tools are allowed, with every applicable licence respected. Acknowledging significant libraries in documentation is encouraged. Teams carry licensing-dispute responsibility. Public frameworks/starter templates are allowed as a foundation; judges evaluate team originality and technical contribution, and minimal original work with excessive template reliance can hurt scoring (5.7-5.8).

Complimentary sponsor resources follow the sponsor's terms. Availability, duration and conditions may change without notice; organiser guarantees neither availability nor functionality (5.9).

### External assistance (5.10)

All significant development must be exclusively by registered members. External parties may not write code, design substantial parts, produce deliverables, prepare presentations, or materially contribute on the team's behalf. Buying development services and outsourcing to third parties are strictly prohibited. The organiser may investigate suspected excessive help.

This should be read together with expressly permitted AI tools (5.6) and organiser-provided workshops/mentoring (4.2.3, 4.5.2). It does not state a detailed threshold distinguishing permitted mentoring/advice from material external contribution.

### Ethics and verification (5.11-5.12)

Projects must comply with applicable laws/regulations. Disallowed content includes violence, hate/discrimination, illegal activities, privacy violations, IP infringement, malware, intentional cybersecurity compromise and organiser-deemed inappropriate material. Personal data in datasets must be lawfully collected/stored/processed. Unlawful, unethical, harmful, offensive or event-inconsistent entries may be rejected/disqualified (5.11).

Technical audits may occur before, during or after judging. Teams must cooperate and provide evidence in the stipulated timeframe or risk penalties/disqualification. Organiser's compliance determination is final unless it decides otherwise (5.12).
