## Preliminary: six required deliverables

Submit through **sdchack.my** before 25 October 2026, **07:59:59 MYT**. (pp. 11-13, 23, 29)

1. **Product Requirement Documentation using the official Submission Template.** Explain the problem, assigned SDG alignment, target users/beneficiaries, scope/boundaries, and key features. Used to assess understanding and real-world impact. (p. 11)
2. **Pitch deck slides.** Problem, solution, technical approach, and expected impact; align with the pitching video's narrative. Preliminary deck file extension is not specified here. (p. 12)
3. **Live/functional prototype access.** Working deployment link or accessible demo build; a video alone or static mockup does not qualify. Judges must interact with or directly observe the actual product. (p. 12)
4. **Technical & Tools Declaration Document.** All major APIs, cloud services, AI tools/LLMs, datasets, models, sponsor resources, pre-existing code/materials. Explain and justify AI-assisted components. Undeclared usage may cause deductions or disqualification. (p. 12)
5. **GitHub/source-code link.** Maintain realistic, continuous commits. A complete/near-complete codebase in one large late commit can be reviewed as suspected prebuilt work and may cause disqualification. Add committee-designated GitHub account(s) as collaborators before the deadline. (p. 13)
6. **Pitching video with prototype demo, 5-7 minutes, YouTube link.** Overtime penalty stated: one point per 30 seconds exceeded, subject to final judging rules. (p. 13)

All submitted work must be created by registered team members. Declare external resources, third-party materials, open-source libraries, datasets, templates, APIs, and AI tools. Late submissions may not be accepted. After the deadline, preliminary materials cannot be modified/resubmitted unless the committee instructs otherwise. (pp. 11, 13)

## Upgrading and final: seven required deliverables

Shortlisted teams can refine prototypes, documents, and presentations in the 28-30 October upgrading round, using competition requirements, judging criteria, and mentor/committee guidance. (p. 15)

Final submission is through **sdchack.my**, due **1 November 2026, 07:59:59 MYT**. (pp. 16, 19)

1. **Updated Product Requirement Documentation.** Reflect refinements, highlight what changed since preliminary and why, including judge/mentor feedback. (p. 16)
2. **Updated Technical & Tools Declaration.** Add APIs, cloud services, AI tools, datasets, sponsor resources, and new explainable AI-assisted components. (p. 16)
3. **Final Solution Report.** Concise written reference covering core features, architecture, SDG impact, and how the solution evolved since preliminary. No word/page limit is specified here. (p. 16)
4. **Backup recorded pitch video, YouTube link.** Standby demonstration for Wi-Fi, deployment, or AV failure; only used if live demo cannot proceed. No separate backup-video duration is specified here. (p. 17)
5. **GitHub/source-code link.** Continuous realistic history; add committee collaborators. The final-stage wording says suspected prebuilt work after review **will** result in disqualification, whereas preliminary says **may**. (p. 17)
6. **Final presentation slides in .pptx.** Cover problem, solution, implementation, SDG relevance, impact, feasibility, and prototype demo. Align with live pitch and verify opening/display before the slot. (p. 18)
7. **Prototype demonstration/live-demo materials.** Working prototype link/build plus files, accounts, or test credentials required to run it; functional and available for judges to interact with or observe. (p. 18)

Teams bear responsibility for broken links, incomplete files, incorrect submissions, and technical issues caused by their preparation. Late final submissions may not be accepted. (p. 19)

## Repositories, licensing, declarations, and required tools

Preparation instructions say repositories should be **private during hacking**, with committee accounts added as collaborators before submission. However, the explicit submission section says the submitted repo **must be public** and remain accessible throughout judging; private repositories may affect eligibility. The practical reading is private while developing, public by submission and through judging, while also adding the designated collaborators. Verify any updated official clarification. (pp. 9, 13, 17, 29)

- The repo must contain an **Apache License 2.0 LICENSE file** and preserve applicable copyright. (p. 29)
- Do not expose API keys, passwords, credentials, or payment details. This requirement appears twice as sections 6.1.6 and 6.1.7. (p. 10)
- Replit **must** be included as part of the solution; other tools/frameworks/services are allowed too. (p. 28)
- All work requiring a GPU must use **RunPod**, expressly including training, fine-tuning, and GPU inference. Other tools/services can be used for non-GPU work. The text does not specifically clarify third-party hosted LLM/API services' internal GPU usage. (p. 28)
- Each participant is allocated **Replit Core, stated value US$20**. Each team receives **US$25 RunPod preliminary credit**. (p. 28)
- Resources are to be accessible through the official website within 24 hours before preliminary; team leads access resources for their members through the portal. (p. 28)
- Replit proof belongs in the Technical & Tools Declaration and must show the team's own account/project. Any one of: live Replit deployment URL, project link giving history access, or the document's exact phrase **"Replit credit finished"**. That last proof type is unclear and should be checked against the declaration template/committee guidance. (p. 29)
- Teams using RunPod must include a dashboard screenshot showing own account, **Pod ID, GPU type, runtime**. If using Serverless, show endpoint URL instead of Pod ID. (p. 29)
- Official templates and video tutorials are in the same Google Drive folder supplied by the user. (p. 30)

## Final pitching and judging

Section 6.7 says **10 minutes pitch plus 5 minutes Q&A**. Overtime penalty: **one point per 30 seconds**, subject to final judging rules. Be ready before the assigned slot. Expected content: problem, solution, implementation, SDG relevance, impact, feasibility, and prototype demonstration. (p. 20)

Teams use their own laptop and must bring an HDMI-capable device/needed adapter (e.g. USB-C to HDMI). The committee does not take responsibility for delays/display/demo failure due to missing adapters, unsupported devices, bad files, or unprepared setup. Teams must bring chargers, multi-plugs, backup equipment, software, and accounts. (p. 21)

Judging decisions are final; individual requests to revisit scores/rankings are not entertained unless the committee verifies an administrative/technical error. Final-score ties are resolved by sub-score priorities in this order: **Technical Execution; Problem-Solution Fit; Impact & Feasibility; Innovation & Creativity; Presentation & Pitch; Design & UX**. Remaining ties go to unanimous panel discussion. Procedures/submissions/judging may be updated through Discord. (p. 22)

## Comparison with Domain 1

1. **Prototype:** consistent. Domain 1 requires live proof of the central working feature; the handbook makes clear preliminary also needs a functional deployed link/build and cannot consist solely of a video or static mockup.
2. **Preliminary demo medium:** Domain 1 says shown live; handbook requires a 5-7 minute recorded YouTube pitch/demo plus interactive/access-to-build deliverable. The combined safe reading is show the real running feature in the video and supply the real accessible product. Whether any synchronous preliminary pitch also occurs is not specified in this handbook.
3. **Tool choice:** Domain 1's free stack choice is qualified by mandatory Replit use and GPU work on RunPod in the handbook. The rest of the stack remains open.
4. **Repository:** Domain 1 permits public or judge-accessible. Handbook section 10 specifically requires public through judging, Apache 2.0, and other sections additionally require collaborator access and continuous commit history.
5. **Data:** Domain 1 is stricter than the generic handbook FAQ: public/open/synthetic only, no real CVs or personal data. Generic FAQ permission for self-collected data cannot be used to bypass that domain boundary.
6. **Disclosure:** Domain 1 requires identifying the LLM and sources for every recommendation. Handbook expands submission disclosure to APIs, services, all tools/models/data, sponsor resources, and pre-existing materials, plus Replit/RunPod proof.
7. **Final:** Domain 1 requires all six functional features; handbook adds seven specific final deliverables and final deadline. Domain 1 says final rubric is different and will be shared with finalists; handbook provides tie-break categories but no final scoring weights.
8. **Missing details filled:** preliminary deadline, six-document/link package, video length, final deadline/package, mandatory tools, licensing, GitHub history/access, travel/setup, mentoring, and communication rules are supplied here.
9. **Still unresolved:** exact preliminary deck/document page or word limits (look to templates), scoring bands, final rubric weights, contradictory final pitch schedule/rooms, and ambiguous "Replit credit finished" proof.
